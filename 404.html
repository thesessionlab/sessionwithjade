The work

Three things break down when you are being watched. I train all three.

Performance coaches tend to work on the outside. Communications people tend to work on the words. Therapists tend to work on the inside and stop there. You are one person, and you need all three in the room at once.

Performance

The problem

You speed up, your voice goes thin, your shoulders rise and you forget the camera is a person. By the eleventh interview of the day you start to look bored.

What changes

A voice and body that hold up when you are tired, and a lens you can talk to like it is one person.

Common issues and outcomes

  • Stiff and self conscious on cameraPresent and easy to watch
  • Rushing and filling every silenceComfortable holding the pause
  • Flat by the end of a press dayThe same energy on the last interview as the first
  • Not knowing where you are in the shotCamera skills you can rely on

Communication

The problem

The question you were hoping to avoid arrives, and you either freeze or say something you did not mean. The clip that travels is never the one you wanted.

What changes

Answers that sound like you, a clear line on what you will not discuss, and a way back to what you came to say.

Common issues and outcomes

  • Explaining something you made instinctivelyTalking about the work without flattening it
  • Hostile or personal questionsStaying steady and saying what you meant
  • Rambling in long interviewsThe point first, in your own words
  • Brand and press lines that sound scriptedMessages that sound like you

Psychology

The problem

Anxiety before, during and for three days afterwards. Imposter feelings that arrive precisely when it goes well. Reading the comments, and what that does to the next one.

What changes

A reset you can use in the ninety seconds before you go on, and recovery, so a bad one does not contaminate the next six.

Common issues and outcomes

  • Fear of exposure and being found outSafe enough to be yourself in public
  • Self doubt in front of certain peopleSteady whoever is in the room
  • Overthinking and rehearsing the wrong thingsPreparing for what actually happens
  • Old patterns from long before the careerPatterns you understand and can change

Press day, 9am

Say you have a junket on Tuesday. Twenty interviews, four minutes each, the same six questions in a slightly different order.

By the eleventh you are tired and answering on autopilot. Someone asks about the thing you did not want to talk about, you laugh it off a bit too hard, and that is the clip that goes round on Wednesday.

The same week, with Jade

  1. 01The Thursday before. We go through the questions you will actually be asked, including the one you are dreading, and find answers that sound like you.
  2. 02Friday. We rehearse on camera. I ask like a tired journalist, I interrupt and I push, then we watch it back the way actors review a take.
  3. 03Monday night. You run the hard answers again on your own in Mara, as many times as you like.
  4. 04Tuesday. A ninety second reset between interviews, so number eleven gets the same person as number one.

What people ask before they book.

Who is Jade Matthew?

Jade Matthew is a London based performance coach and working actress. She has twenty years of screen and radio credits across the BBC, HBO, Netflix, Channel 4, ITV and Sky, a BBC Audio Drama Award, and she lectures at LAMDA. She is also a clinical hypnotherapist and Master NLP Practitioner. Through Session with Jade she coaches actors, musicians, public figures, influencers and the people who represent them.

What is Session with Jade?

Session with Jade is one to one performance, presence and media coaching for people in the public eye. It covers how you come across on camera, in interviews, on stage and online, and how you handle the pressure of being watched. The work combines performance training, communication and psychology in one approach, the ICOP Method.

How is this different from media training?

Media training usually works on the words: key messages and how to steer away from a question. I work on the words, the delivery and what is happening underneath at the same time, so your answer still holds up on the eleventh interview of the day, or when someone asks the question you were hoping to avoid.

Do you prepare people for press junkets and interviews?

Yes. Press and campaign preparation covers releases, tours, launches and announcements. We work on the questions you will actually be asked, rehearse on camera under conditions close to the real day, and build a ninety second reset you can use between interviews.

Do you work with musicians and artists?

Yes. Album press, documentary interviews, talking between songs on stage, and talking about work that is personal without giving away more than you want to.

Do you coach influencers and content creators?

Yes, through personal brand coaching. We work on on camera presence, knowing what you stand for and saying it clearly, live streams and podcasts, press and brand partnerships, and handling comments and criticism without it changing how you show up.

Can you help with performance anxiety and stage fright?

Yes, as coaching rather than medical treatment. Alongside the performance work I use clinical hypnotherapy, NLP and breathwork to help with nerves before appearances, imposter feelings and the fear of being judged in public. If you have a diagnosed condition, I am happy to work alongside your GP or therapist.

Is it confidential?

Completely. I sign NDAs and production agreements before we start, I never name clients or confirm who I work with, and I never use the work as content. Rehearsal footage belongs to you and is deleted on request.

Do managers, agents and publicists book you?

Yes. Either for one client ahead of a specific campaign, or on a roster retainer with an agreed number of days each month and short notice availability when something lands tomorrow.

Where are you based, and do you work online?

I am based in London and work in person there, and online with people anywhere in the world, fitted around filming and touring schedules where I can.

How much does it cost?

Fees are on application and quoted in writing after a free 20 minute call. Campaign preparation is a fixed fee agreed in advance, retainers are priced on days per month, and one to one coaching runs in blocks of six to ten sessions.

What is the ICOP Method?

The ICOP Method is the four stage framework Jade Matthew developed from twenty years of performing and coaching. Identify: understand what is actually happening. Clear: work on the patterns, beliefs and responses getting in your way. Own: build practical tools to change your state and behaviour on purpose. Perform: rehearse the real situation until you respond differently when it matters.
